## Decision

**The arithmetic reconciles, but the affordable plan remains an unquoted target.** The $32,139 venue envelope is available only if all $22,406 of other allocations are met. Spending the entire venue ceiling leaves just $0.45 below the $54,545.45 spending limit. Keep the $5,454.55 reserve untouched and prefer a complete venue-driven total at or below $31,139 so at least $1,000 remains unallocated inside the spend limit.

The most useful improvement is a fully itemized, tax-inclusive budget tied to 2027 written quotes, rather than another round of across-the-board cuts. Protect the indoor room plan, adequate food and service, sound, and coordination. Use real savings from simplified décor or genuinely duplicated inclusions to improve those priorities after prices are established.

## Ranked improvements to estimates

1. **Make the venue quote complete and use its scope, not its headline per-person price.** Price all ceremony/holding/cocktail rooms, room flip, setup and strike, food, bar for the full agreed service duration, mandatory staff, service/admin charges, taxes, rentals, cake/cutting, getting-ready room, parking, permits and security. Unknowns remain unknown; a zero entered for an unpublished fee is not free. Compare complete packages and complete hall-plus-catering totals.
2. **Quote the biggest unverified non-venue allocations before allocating any headroom.** Décor $5,446, attire/beauty $4,375, coordinator $2,500, tips $2,000, DJ/sound $2,000 and video $1,800 are still targets. Request the exact required scope, tax, travel, assistants, overtime and delivery/collection. No amount is safe to reallocate merely because an advertised starting price is lower.
3. **Resolve sales tax and fee treatment outside the venue calculation.** Non-venue caps are not automatically taxed by the model. Treat every cap as a delivered, tax-inclusive maximum. For example, the original aggregate tax line included tax on the $935 specialty linens/chargers; the current model does not separately add tax to that fixed line. At 6%, a pre-tax $935 quote would need another $56.10. This is a scope ambiguity until the quote establishes whether $935 is already all-in. Check cake, attire, rings, florals, hotel and other independent purchases similarly rather than assuming one universal rate.
4. **Replace percentage-based tips with a recipient and contract ledger.** A service/admin charge is not automatically staff gratuity. Identify required gratuities already included in proposals and intended extra tips by role. Remove a duplicate only when the contract demonstrates it; the current $2,000 is an allocation, not an established bill. Conversely, any mandatory gratuity above it must be funded inside the spend limit.
5. **Resolve rental and décor overlap explicitly.** Basic linens and specialty linens/chargers may represent distinct products or duplicate full linens. Ask whether specialty cost is an upgrade difference or the full replacement charge. Candles already mention setup/strike, while florist delivery/setup is another line: establish responsibilities and avoid paying twice for the same labor. Included venue tables, china, dance floor or cake cutting already removed from a venue scenario cannot become new savings a second time.
6. **Confirm the actual table and vendor counts.** Nineteen tables assumes ten guests per table. At eight guests per table, 185 guests require 24 tables: five more trimmed centerpieces add $500; the model's 12% florist assumption adds $60; the specialty-linen table component adds $100. Basic linens add another $150 if separately rented, before tax. These are mechanical sensitivities, not a proposed seating change. Price the approved floor plan. Vendor meals currently assume approximately ten at $40; replace with the actual required crew count, meal price and taxes/fees, avoiding a second count inside catering.
7. **Track cash due separately from total cost.** Deposits are payments against a contract, not extra expenses. Refundable damage deposits can require additional temporary cash even if refunded later. Record due dates and refundable status. Do not treat the untouched reserve as deposit funding or silently count expected gifts as available cash.

## What can and cannot improve the wedding without increasing the budget

Keep money first for a comfortable indoor event, enough staff to serve 185 guests, clear ceremony/speech sound, a competent DJ and adequate coordination. Avoid buying visual upgrades before floor plans and service scope are known. A naturally attractive room, standard included linens, a concentrated floral focal point and reusable ceremony arrangements can preserve the appearance, but any reuse labor must be priced and the couple must accept changes to current required choices.

A venue coordinator replaces the $2,500 independent-coordination allocation only if the written scope actually covers the required couple/vendor timeline, rehearsal, setup supervision and full event. An included hotel night can replace the separate $600 room; it does not automatically replace getting-ready access. A DJ bundle can remove the separate $500 sound allocation only when all rooms, microphones, equipment, staffing and hours are included. Confirmed savings remain unallocated until outstanding quotes fit.

Skipping favors, transport, rehearsal dinner, photo booth, album, uplighting and other optional extras produces **no new saving** against the current plan; they are already budgeted at zero. A fee-free lot is a venue benefit, but savings exist only relative to a scenario that currently charges parking.

A venue total of $30,000 would leave $2,139.45 unallocated if all other caps hold. This is an internal shopping target, not a claim that any venue is available at that price. Likewise a hypothetical 4% increase on a $31,139 quote adds $1,245.56; historical quotes need the required 2027 uplift before comparison. Never apply that uplift again to a firm 2027 price.

## Confirmed model limitations and proposed fixes

No model files were changed by this review. Existing embedded model tests passed.

- **Must-have policy failures can receive a fit label.** A synthetic package with adequate capacity and other positive fields, but candles prohibited, outside vendors prohibited and a 21:00 end time, returns `FITS as-is`. Those three fields are currently reported under `nice_to_check`, not enforced. Proposed fix: explicitly reject known failures and mark unknown policies unresolved. Heat, holding-space capacity and permission to serve alcohol also need explicit verification.
- **Fit labels do not implement the current $1,000 headroom rule.** The model compares against its mathematical envelope ($32,139.45), not the stricter $32,139 policy ceiling and fits/edge distinction. Proposed fix: keep arithmetic results, but apply a separate decision policy with fits at $31,139 or lower, edge through $32,139, no above that or for failed requirements, and unknown for missing price/capacity or unresolved evidence.
- **Reserve-spending alternatives remain in the CLI.** The model displays a second reading that spends the $5,454.55 reserve. It is disallowed by the locked plan. Proposed fix: omit it from recommendations and the decision verdict; retain only if clearly identified as an unused legacy diagnostic.
- **Included cake can incorrectly suppress a separate cutting fee.** The cutting calculation is nested beneath “cake not included.” A package with cake included but cutting charged would omit cutting. No current saved output spec was found with cake included and cutting omitted, so this is a latent defect, not a demonstrated correction to an existing venue total. Proposed fix: test cake and cake-cutting inclusion separately.
- **A price year does not make a number a quote.** Unpublished-package solver ceilings and neutral aggregate-catering scenarios can be numerically valid while remaining unquoted. Keep provenance, effective price year, quote status, scope and uncertainty alongside every total; do not use the CLI fit string as proof of availability or affordability.
