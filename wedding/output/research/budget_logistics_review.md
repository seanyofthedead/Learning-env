# Budget and guest-experience review

Reviewed November 8, 2026. This is an adversarial check of the existing plan, not a new quote or permission to change the requirements. The baseline stays at 185 guests, the stated November 2027 dates, hosted beer and wine, $54,545.45 maximum spending and an untouched $5,454.55 reserve. Category caps stay locked. Nobody has been contacted.

**The main exposure is missing work and missing space, rather than a single overlooked purchase.** An inexpensive hall can require a second room, a longer rental, more staff and extra deliveries. A low food price does not establish a comfortable, fully serviced wedding. None of the ranked venues is a confirmed fit yet.

## Resolve these before paying a venue deposit

| Risk | Evidence needed | Low-cost protection and decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Dinner capacity does not include dancing or service areas | A dimensioned plan with 185 seats at rounds, retained dance floor, DJ, buffet, cake, bar, accessible aisles and emergency exits | Have the venue and caterer approve the same plan. No removing dinner tables to create the required dance floor. General banquet capacity is insufficient. |
| Ceremony or flip displaces guests | Named heated indoor ceremony room for 185, or a priced flip with a holding room for everyone; named separate cocktail space | Draw where all guests are at every stage. Confirm room exclusivity and flip staff. A corridor, small coat room or tented deck is not evidence of the required heated ceremony/holding solution. |
| November weather exposes guests during transitions | Route from parking through ceremony, cocktails, dinner and restrooms; indoor cold/rain alternative for any outdoor cocktail plan | Prefer an already included indoor route and coat storage. Indoor cocktails are a practical protection, not a newly invented locked requirement. If the plan requires heaters, tents, flooring or umbrellas, obtain real costs and fit them inside the existing caps before accepting it. |
| Advertised event hours exclude necessary labor | One timeline naming vendor arrival, getting ready, ceremony, cocktail hour, dinner, music/bar end, guest departure, strike and final vehicle departure | Quote the whole access window. Example only: 2–5pm setup, 5–10pm guest program, 10pm–midnight strike is ten hours; an 11pm guest finish makes eleven. The venue and suppliers must approve actual durations. Avoid an evening that ends early merely because cleanup is inside the rental. |
| Coordinator or venue staff are assumed to do physical work | Written task ownership for unloading, furniture, table setting, florals, flip, bussing, washing, trash, packing personal items and late collection | “Supervision” and “coordination” do not establish a labor crew. Allocate each task once to a paid supplier or a specifically willing helper; do not make essential service depend on guests missing the wedding. Confirm coordinator hours reach the final handoff. |
| Low catering quote leaves queues or cleanup gaps | Staffing by role and paid hours; buffet release plan; bar locations; hot/cold holding; replenishment; allergy process; dish clearing and final cleanup | Ask the caterer to show how 185 people are served within the meal schedule. Request two-sided or parallel service only where space and quoted staffing support it. Do not invent a universal servers-to-guests ratio. Confirm whether the kitchen only warms food or supports cooking. |
| Shared facilities reduce usable capacity | Restroom locations and availability, accessible route, elevator operation if relevant, accessible parking, and who else uses the building | Walk the entire route on the tour. Ask the venue to confirm lawful occupancy for the actual arrangement and expected restroom demand. Upstairs capacity or general ADA wording alone does not prove the event's route works. |
| Outside vendors or candles trigger a late redesign | Written DJ, florist, baker, enclosed-candle and alcohol permissions, with insurance and fee requirements | Get permission before buying décor or paying the relevant vendor. Do not substitute LED candles or alter another locked requirement as an assumed fix. |

A contract should identify which rooms and equipment are included, rather than rely on “access to the venue.” If the complete design cannot meet the requirements inside the caps, the venue remains unqualified even when the rent itself is low.

## The table count can change costs without changing guest count

The model assumes ten guests per table: 185 guests means **19 tables**. The Gathering Place's [official details page](https://thegatheringplacemd.net/details/) says a 60-inch round seats eight comfortably and a 72-inch round seats ten; that page is undated. These are that venue's published descriptions, not a universal occupancy standard. Ask every venue for actual table sizes and the approved floor plan.

At eight guests per table, the same 185 guests need **24 tables**. Under the existing model formulas, five additional tables imply this arithmetic sensitivity:

| Existing allowance/formula | Additional cost from five tables |
|---|---:|
| Trimmed centerpieces: $100 per table | $500 |
| Florist fee: 12% of those additional centerpieces | $60 |
| Basic linen table component: $30 per table | $150, before applicable rental tax |
| Specialty-linen table component: $20 per table | $100 |
| **Total before additional rental tax** | **$810** |

This is not a revised venue price, quote or authorized increase. The model's per-guest chair, china and napkin quantities do not change in this illustration; actual round-table inventory, delivery volume and rental charges may. A sweetheart/head table, buffet, cocktail, cake and gift tables also need their own linen count. Ask for an inventory rather than assuming the dining-table count covers every surface. Keep the approved category caps by revising a quoted design within them; do not silently use the reserve or move money between categories.

## Count people, meals and coverage separately

**Define the 185 once.** Confirm whether it includes the couple, children and wedding party, then reconcile the invitation list, seating plan and caterer's guaranteed guest count. Keep 185 as the pricing baseline. Do not rely on expected declines, children eating free or an unapproved smaller guest list.

The workbook separately allows **$400 for roughly ten vendor meals**. Get each contract's meal requirement, count the actual workers and request the meal price inclusive of applicable charges. Ten videography/photography/DJ/coordinator/other crew members is an estimate, not a confirmed count. Establish whether vendors eat separately, need seats in the reception layout, and receive dinner at a time that preserves coverage of speeches and events. Do not count them both in 185 full-price guests and again in the vendor-meal line.

The **$500 ceremony/cocktail sound allowance** sits beside the **$1,500 DJ allowance**. Ask whether the DJ package includes both rooms, microphones, technician time, power and the full schedule. Included venue speakers do not establish microphone coverage or somebody to operate them. If the DJ includes a function, remove only a documented duplicate; do not assume the allowance is free to spend elsewhere.

Video starting packages range from shorter coverage to eight hours in the [vendor checks](../vendor_checks.md). Set a desired sequence—preparation, ceremony, portraits, speeches and dancing—before choosing hours. The booked photographer's schedule, date-change terms and coverage scope must also be confirmed. Plan portraits and family groups against the actual November daylight and indoor backup; avoid buying overtime to repair an impossible timeline. Nobody has promised free additional coverage.

## Attire, beauty and small expenses need complete quotes

The **$4,375 attire/beauty cap** is already allocated: dress $1,200, alterations $400, accessories $300, bride's shoes $150, suit/tux $300, groom's accessories $200, bands $1,200, hair/makeup $500 and trials $125. These are allowances, not confirmed tax-inclusive purchases.

For each purchase or booking, confirm tax, delivery, alterations, steaming, rental return charges, artist travel/parking, minimum services and assistant or early-start fees where applicable. Do not assume wedding-party beauty services, outfits or gifts are hosted by the couple; they are not funded in this baseline. Confirm the couple's intended scope before making any commitment. Request an itemized quote that fits the existing line/category, and avoid adding new speculative allowances on top.

The **$200 emergency/miscellaneous line** should remain for modest incidental needs. Predictable staffing, transport, delivery, permits or contract charges belong in their own complete quotes. The **$50 marriage-license allowance** remains an estimate until the ceremony jurisdiction, residency rule and payment method are known; do not assume the home county dictates where the license must be purchased.

## A zero transport or pre/post budget still needs a plan

A zero host-paid guest-transport budget does not mean transport is included. Before choosing a venue, confirm parking quantity, cost, accessible drop-off, nighttime exit lighting, vehicle retrieval rules and a workable pickup point. Provide guests with accurate parking and travel details. Do not advertise a hotel shuttle unless the hotel confirms its route, capacity, hours and reservation terms in writing. A courtesy room block should have no couple-paid minimum, guarantee or attrition liability before it can be treated as zero cost.

For the couple, identify who transports attire, décor, alcohol if permitted, personal bags and gifts; who drives home or to the wedding-night room; and who collects items after strike. A sober pickup plan and willing drivers are logistical assignments, not an assumption of free hired transport. If the venue's location makes a paid shuttle necessary for the intended experience, it has an unfunded dependency: resolve that inside the existing approved budget before booking.

The **$0 pre/post-events cap** means no promised hosted rehearsal dinner, welcome party or farewell brunch. A ceremony rehearsal can still require room access, coordinator time and an officiant; ask whether that work is included and price any requirement. Use an included venue walkthrough/rehearsal slot where practical. Do not let an optional gathering become a contractual or social commitment without funding. The brother's stationery/postage gift is locked as a gift in the workbook; verify timing and deliverables without reallocating imaginary savings.

## Cash timing is separate from expense

Maintain two ledgers:

- **Expense forecast:** all contracted costs plus complete estimates for remaining commitments, by the existing caps. A booking deposit credited to the final bill is a payment toward that expense, not an additional expense.
- **Cash schedule:** deposits, later installments, card authorizations and refundable security deposits by due date, plus the expected refund date. A refundable deposit is not an expense unless retained, but it still needs available cash. A credit-card hold can also reduce usable funds.

The Gathering Place's [official terms](https://thegatheringplacemd.net/details/) require full nonrefundable rent to secure a date, with a 24-hour hold after a tour. Other venues have different terms. Compare cash due and cancellation exposure before treating two similarly priced options as equivalent. Never count an expected refund as cash already available for another payment.

Keep the **$5,454.55 reserve untouched**, including temporary deposit financing. Before signing, confirm the necessary payment cash exists outside that reserve. Request schedules, refunds, rescheduling rights, damage terms, payment-method surcharges and the procedure for approving overtime or extras. Name one authorized decision-maker and require advance written approval for discretionary additions.

The workbook's **$700 insurance total** is $300 liability plus $400 cancellation, not one interchangeable pool. Obtain the actual policy quote and read effective dates, exclusions, deductibles, cancellation reasons, vendor failure and deposit coverage before relying on it. Follow the planned insurance-before-signing sequence; buying a policy does not establish that every nonrefundable payment is covered.
