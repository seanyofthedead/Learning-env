# Flowers, décor, attire and beauty: protect the look and price the work

Prepared 8 November 2026. **Planning alternatives only: no budget changes accepted, vendors contacted or purchases made.** Keep 185 guests and the sage / stone / ivory palette. These allocations use the model's current 19-table assumption; the approved floor plan may require more tables. Keep the $5,454.55 wedding reserve untouched.

The best improvement is to protect fit, personal flowers, lighting and paid setup while simplifying the number of custom designs. A small coordinated arrangement on every table can carry the palette. A cheap collection of products without delivery, placement and removal is not a complete wedding service.

## Where to spend first

1. Pay for correct dress/suit fit, a trusted beauty trial and durable, comfortable bands before decorative accessory upgrades.
2. Keep the bouquet and ceremony focal point deliberate, then repeat one modest table design across all 19 tables. Preserve color, consistency and usable table space rather than buying 19 different installations.
3. Obtain actual setup, flip and strike quotes before reducing labor. Using the same flowers twice saves materials only when the timing, access and paid handling work.
4. Keep the $411 floral allowance uncommitted until delivery/strike and tax are written down. A future under-run is a possible saving; none is confirmed today.

The caps can support a restrained, coordinated design and careful shopping, but the available sources do not establish a fully quoted plan at these totals. The biggest risks are a full floral arch, full-service florist labor, beauty minimums/peak-season charges, and garment or band choices that exceed the shopping targets. No source-backed saving beyond the existing caps is claimed here.
