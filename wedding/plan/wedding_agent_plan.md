## 2. What the budget means for finding a place

### Spend limit

- **Reading 2:** the reserve sits on top, so the limit is $60,000. That adds **$5,455** to every envelope and leaves no contingency.
- **You chose Reading 1** with 185 guests and trims, so the target is **$32,139 all-in** for the venue, food, drink and rentals.
- Costs outside the venue are **$31,475** at 185 guests. At the workbook's estimates, everything totals $76,658.

### The venue envelope (Reading 1)

The envelope covers everything the venue choice drives:
- rental and ceremony space
- food, staff, bar, service charge and tax
- cake
- rentals the venue doesn't supply
- getting-ready room and parking
- extra hours and required fees

### The all-in rule

Compare venues on all-in cost, never on the headline rental. Take a fire hall that rents for **$3,000** on a Saturday:

- **$4,056:** the hall in 2027 (2 extra hours, the outside-caterer fee, +4% for price increases).
- **$6,031:** linens, china, cake, cake cutting, delivery, getting-ready room and setup labor.
- **$38,016:** the workbook's mid-range caterer at about $205/guest × 185. That's food $95 + staff $25 + alcohol $35 + soft drinks $4, then 20% service, 6% tax and bartenders.
- **Total: about $48,100, which is $16K over the trimmed envelope.**

With a budget caterer and a beer-and-wine bar (about $92/guest), the same hall comes to about **$27,150**. That fits.

- "DC–NoVA" means DC, Fairfax County, Arlington and Alexandria. "MD" also covers VA areas with no meals tax.
- If a venue publishes only 2026 prices, divide the package ceilings by 1.04.
- If the hall has its own tables and chairs, add about $11/guest to the rental-hall ceiling.
- **Tax by location:**
  - DC: 10% on catering.
  - Fairfax County: 10%, outside its towns.
  - Arlington and Alexandria: 11%.
  - Maryland: 6%, and 9% on alcohol. The alcohol rate may now be 12%; to confirm.

### Venue must-haves

If a venue lacks one of these, its cost gets priced in or the venue is rejected.

- [ ] Seats the guest count **at round tables with a dance floor**. If not, reject.
- [ ] Indoor ceremony space: a separate room, or a priced room flip with a holding room.
- [ ] Separate cocktail-hour space.
- [ ] Tables & chairs (about $10/guest if missing, an assumption) and setup labor (about $3/guest).
- [ ] Getting-ready space ($600 if missing).
- [ ] Parking: a free lot for about 75 cars, or about $15/car, or a $1,500 shuttle.
- [ ] Dance floor ($1,200), linens (about $850) and china ($1,480), each priced in if missing.
- [ ] All spaces indoors and heated.
- Also check: enclosed candles allowed, outside vendors allowed, end time 11pm or later, and alcohol rules.

## 3. The agent

### Day to day

- **6:13am, reader:** reads replies in Gmail (read-only) and pulls out dates, prices, holds and tour times. Instructions found inside emails are flagged, never followed.
- **7:13am, planner:** updates prices, drafts follow-ups, adds confirmed tours to a Wedding calendar, and sends you a "Today" card.
- **You, 20–40 minutes a day:** tap Send, make 1–3 calls, run one marketplace search.
- **7:41pm weekdays, scout:** checks public and community websites for new venues, a few pages at a time.
- **Sunday:** a weekly digest.
- **Monday:** a safety check. The agent tries to send an email, open Peerspace and invite someone to an event. All three must fail.

### The venue loop

1. **Plan:** turn the envelope into price ceilings. Redo this whenever the guest count, date or budget reading changes.
2. **Sweep:** check park, city, fire-hall, fraternal, parish, university, golf and hotel sites, plus tourism wedding desks. Every fact is saved with its URL and date.
3. **Marketplace searches:** you run these (see section 4).
4. **Screen in code:** reject a venue that is:
   - too small or outdoor-only
   - without alcohol, or ending before 10pm
   - over 60 minutes away, or on your exclusion list

   A venue with unknowns is marked "pending", never counted as a fit.
5. **Price all-in** at 185, 165 and 150 guests.
6. **Inquire:** up to 10 drafts a day. Each asks about Sat 13 Nov 2027 first, then Sat 6, Sat 20 and Fri 12 Nov. Sun 14 comes up only as a last resort.
7. **Caterer quotes:** request 3–5 for each hall that passes screening.
8. **Chase:** nudge after 3 business days. If that fails, you get a call script.
9. **Tour** the confirmed fits.
10. **Decide:** you buy insurance, then sign. The agent drafts polite releases to the other venues.

### The vendor loop

The same steps run for each category: a brief with the cap, a search, an all-in price, and at most 3 new vendors per round.

- **Now:** coordinator and videographer inquiries.
- **After signing:** DJ, officiant and hotel block.
- **Rule:** nothing tied to the date is booked until the venue and your photographer both confirm it.

## 4. Using Peerspace and your other logins

### Peerspace playbook

- **Terms:**
  - Robots are banned "for any purpose".
  - You're liable for activity on your account "whether or not authorized".
  - A deal moved off the platform owes Peerspace **30%**.
  - The **full total** is charged when the host accepts.
- **Fit:** listed capacity is usually standing room, not seated.
  - At 185 guests, a reception fits only at about $200/hr or less, and only with trims and lean catering.
  - At 150, the limit is about $650/hr.
  - Both are a little under the section 2 table, to leave room for platform fees.
- **How to use it:**
  - Run one 30-minute reception search as a test.
  - After that, use Peerspace for side rooms: a getting-ready suite (about $90/hr or less), rehearsal space, the rehearsal dinner, or a ceremony-only room.
- **The search brief:**
  - Wedding, one area per run.
  - Sat 13 Nov 2027, 1pm–midnight.
  - Exact guest count and a maximum hourly rate.
  - Keywords: "banquet", "dance floor", "outside catering".
  - Instant Book off.
- **Paste back** 10–15 serious listings per search, using the agent's template.
- **Messages stay on Peerspace.** Say it's a wedding and give the real headcount. Ask for the venue's address so the agent can check it against your exclusion list.
- **Book** only with a written floor plan and a cancellation policy of "Standard 90 Day" or better. Cancel only through the platform.

### Credential rule

- The agent never sees a password, cookie, 2FA code or card number. If you paste one, it refuses to store it and asks you to change it.
- You log in to your own accounts yourselves.
- Use a wedding-only Google account. Its security emails go to a label the agent can't read.

### Approval gates

- **Never possible:** sending, logging in, booking, signing, paying, inviting people to events, scraping.
- **One tap (either of you):** every draft, every pasted message, tour times.
- **Both of you:**
  - the guest plan, the budget reading, alternate dates and the radius
  - dropping a must-have, or sharing a budget figure
  - any hold that carries a fee
  - choosing and signing the venue, and every deposit
  - moving money between caps
- **Red flags:** new payment details or off-platform requests. Check payment details by phone, using the number on the venue's own website.
